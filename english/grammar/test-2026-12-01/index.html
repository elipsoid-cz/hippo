Grammar rules

much / many / enough

  • many + things you can count: many apples, many people, many questions
  • much + things you can't count: much milk, much time, much money, much homework
  • too many / too much = more than is good
  • enough = as much as you need · not enough = less than you need
  • Watch out: enough goes after an adjective: tall enough, but before a noun: enough money

Past simple

  • Statement: I played, I went (regular verbs add -ed, irregular verbs you have to learn)
  • Question: Did + base form: Did you cook yesterday?
  • Negative: didn't + base form: I didn't swim yesterday.
  • After did / didn't, never use went, swam, found!

Present perfect

  • have / has + past participle (3rd form): I have seen, she has eaten
  • he / she / it → has, everyone else → have
  • Negative: I haven't finished · Question: Have you ever been…?

Present perfect or past simple?

Past simple (we say when)Present perfect (when is not important)
yesterday, last week, last summer, two days ago, in 2020, when…? ever, never, already, yet, just, since, for
I went to London last summer.I have been to London.

“Have you ever…?” is present perfect. When you say when it happened, switch to past simple: Yes, I went there last summer.
